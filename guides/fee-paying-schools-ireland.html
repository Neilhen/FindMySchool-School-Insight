Fee-paying schools in Ireland

Ireland has 51 fee-paying secondary schools and 8 fee-paying junior/prep schools. They operate under a unique hybrid model where the State pays standard teacher salaries while parents pay tuition for extra staffing, smaller senior classes, specialized facilities, and boarding. Here is how they work, what they actually cost, and how admission is decided.

The State subsidy: how fee-paying schools work in Ireland

Unlike independent private schools in Britain or the United States, recognised fee-paying secondary schools in Ireland are State-subsidised. Under the Department of Education's post-primary staffing schedule, the State pays the basic salaries of teachers up to the standard staffing quota (currently set at a pupil-teacher allocation ratio of 19:1).

What tuition fees pay for is everything on top: privately employed additional teachers (allowing a wider subject choice and smaller classes for the Leaving Certificate), state-of-the-art sports facilities (astroturf pitches, swimming pools, rowing pavilions), dedicated science labs and music wings, grounds upkeep, and campus administration.

Day vs. boarding schools

Of Ireland's 51 fee-paying secondary schools, 16 offer boarding — either 5-day weekly boarding or full 7-day boarding. Boarding schools include Clongowes Wood College, Glenstal Abbey, St Columba's College, Rockwell College, Cistercian College Roscrea, The King's Hospital, Midleton College, Bandon Grammar, Rathdown School, Alexandra College, Dundalk Grammar, Monaghan Collegiate, Villiers School, and Sligo Grammar.

Tuition fees differ substantially between day and boarding pupils:

  • Day pupils: Published tuition fees for day students typically range between €4,600 and €11,500 per year, with the average Dublin day school charging around €7,000 to €8,500.
  • Boarding pupils: Boarding fees are all-inclusive of tuition, residential accommodation, catering, evening study supervision, and weekend activities. These range from €15,000 to over €26,500 per year.

The true total cost: beyond the headline fee

The published tuition fee is rarely the full bill. Families budgeting for a private secondary education should anticipate several mandatory or near-mandatory ancillary charges:

Annual and one-off surcharges

  • Capital and development leviesMany schools charge an annual building/capital fund levy (typically €500 to €1,500 per family or pupil) or a non-refundable entry bond.
  • Transition Year (TY) surchargeFourth year involves trips, outdoor education, work experience modules, and guest workshops, typically adding €800 to €2,500 to the standard tuition in that year.
  • Compulsory dining schemesSeveral day schools (such as Belvedere College or Blackrock College) operate mandatory or near-universal dining halls with lunch fees running €800–€1,500 annually.

Equipment and uniform

  • 1:1 Digital device leasingMost fee-paying schools now mandate a 1:1 iPad or laptop programme, leased through the school or purchased with approved educational management software (€600–€1,200).
  • Specialist sports kitMandatory blazers, formal uniforms, and specialist kit for rugby, hockey, rowing, cricket, or athletics frequently add €500–€1,000+ in first year.
  • State examination & mock feesNon-DEIS and fee-paying pupils pay State exam fees and school mock exam correction charges.

Admissions, waiting lists, and the 25% past-pupil cap

Until recently, private schools in Ireland often registered children at birth, meaning places were locked up years before a child reached secondary age. The Education (Admission to Schools) Act 2018 transformed this landscape:

  1. Waiting lists years in advance are illegal.A school cannot compile a waiting list prior to the year before admission. Applications must open during a designated annual window (typically October of 6th class).
  2. The 25% past-pupil cap.By law, a school may reserve at most 25% of its available places for children or grandchildren of past pupils. The remaining 75%+ must be allocated under published criteria.
  3. No interview or academic selection.Recognised schools receiving State funding may not interview children or require entrance tests to determine admission. (Assessment tests after admission are solely for class placement).

Because fee-paying schools are heavily oversubscribed, the non-legacy places are usually decided by strict criteria: siblings already in the school receive top priority, followed by designated feeder primary schools, parish or church affiliation (where relevant to ethos), and finally a public lottery.

The junior school pipeline

Several prominent fee-paying secondary colleges have direct sister or feeder preparatory schools:

  • Willow Park Junior School → Blackrock College
  • St Michael's Junior School → St Michael's College (Senior)
  • St Conleth's Junior School → St Conleth's College (Senior)
  • Sandford Park Junior → Sandford Park School (Senior)
  • Alexandra Junior School → Alexandra College (Senior)
  • The Teresian Primary → The Teresian School (Senior)
  • St Mary's Junior School → St Mary's College, Rathmines
  • Rathgar Junior School → The High School

While attending the junior school provides an established pathway into the secondary school, junior schools receive no State salary support and charge independent primary tuition fees (typically €5,000 to €9,000 a year).

Scholarships and bursaries

Several fee-paying schools offer academic, musical, or means-tested financial support:

  • Academic & Music Scholarships: Schools such as Alexandra College, The High School, Wesley College, Gonzaga, and Midleton College offer partial tuition scholarships based on annual entrance scholarship exams or musical auditions.
  • Means-Tested Bursaries: Most notably, Belvedere College SJ runs the Social Diversity Programme (SDP), which provides fully funded secondary education to roughly 10% to 15% of its student body from families who could not otherwise afford fees. Other schools with charitable trusts or religious endowments offer confidential fee assistance to families facing unexpected financial hardship.
