DEIS schools in Ireland

1,197 schools are in the DEIS programme — but DEIS is not one thing. It has four bands, and they do not get the same supports. The band is the part that decides what your child actually receives, and it is the part almost nobody publishes.

Is a DEIS school a worse school?

No — and the question has the logic backwards. DEIS status is not a judgement passed on a school. It is a measure of the community the school serves, and it results in more resources going in, not fewer.

A school is designated DEIS because of the socio-economic profile of the families enrolled in it, assessed by the Department. Nothing in that assessment measures teaching, leadership or outcomes. Two schools a mile apart can differ in DEIS status purely because of who lives around them.

The practical consequence runs the other way from the assumption. A DEIS Urban Band 1 junior class is capped at 17 pupils. The ordinary primary staffing schedule is well above that. If small classes are what you are looking for, Band 1 schools are among the smallest classes available anywhere in the State, fee-paying schools included.

What DEIS gets you — and what everyone now gets anyway

This is where most published advice is out of date, and it matters because families choose schools on it.

Still specific to DEIS

  • Reduced class sizesUrban Band 1 only. 17:1 junior, 19:1 vertical, 21:1 senior.
  • Home School Community Liaison coordinatorA named member of staff whose job is the link between home and school.
  • School Completion ProgrammeTargeted work with pupils at risk of leaving education early.
  • Extra staffing and enhanced capitationMore teachers and more money per pupil than the standard schedule.
  • Free State exam feesAt DEIS post-primary schools.

Now in every school

  • Free schoolbooksUniversal at primary since 2023 and at post-primary since 2024, DEIS or not.
  • Hot school mealsSince 2025 every primary school is eligible, not only DEIS schools.
  • Free period productsBeing extended to all schools by 2027 under the new DEIS strategy.

If you are choosing a school because DEIS means free books or a hot meal, that reasoning no longer separates one school from another. The staffing does.

DEIS Plus

In March 2026 the Department announced DEIS Plus: €48 million a year for 121 schools with the most concentrated and persistent disadvantage — 96 primary and 25 post-primary — chosen on a refined identification model looking at three years of data.

Those schools get additional teachers including mental-health coordinators, administrative principals, guidance counsellors in primary schools, full-time HSCL coordinators, wellbeing practitioners, breakfast clubs and an innovation fund. Staff begin arriving in the second half of 2026. It sits alongside a ten-year DEIS strategy running to 2035.

We do not yet flag which 121 schools these are. The Department has not published the list in a form we can read and join reliably, and we would rather say that than guess. If you know of an official list, tell us.

How a school gets into DEIS

Schools do not apply. The Department identifies them centrally, using the socio-economic profile of the pupils actually enrolled, drawn from its own returns and the Pobal HP Deprivation Index for the areas those pupils come from.

The programme was extended in 2022, bringing in 279 of the schools listed here. That expansion is why some schools you remember as non-DEIS now carry it.
